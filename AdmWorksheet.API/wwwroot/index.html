STEP 02 OF 04 · SELF-ASSESSMENT
IMSAFE CHECKLIST
Rate each factor as Low / Medium / High risk. Any single "High" rating is a potential Go/No-Go decision point per the PAVE framework under "Pilot."
I ILLNESS
Evaluate current health issues that could impair judgment or physical performance. Ask: "Do I have symptoms (fever, congestion, dizziness) that could worsen under flight stress?"
M MEDICATION
List every medication and check FAA-approved aeromedical guidance. Consider: drowsiness, blurred vision, nausea. Even "mild" meds can raise severity in the GRM matrix.
S STRESS
High stress drains cognitive bandwidth. Rate: low (manageable), medium (occasional distraction), high (constant preoccupation). In GRM: high stress + marginal weather = unacceptable risk.
A ALCOHOL
FAA mandates at least 8 hours "bottle to throttle," but residual effects may linger longer. Enforce personal minimums beyond regulatory minimums. If in doubt → High risk.
F FATIGUE
Fatigue undermines every phase of flight — treat it as seriously as any mechanical failure. Note hours slept in the past 24 hours and quality of rest. Revisit at top-of-descent.
E EMOTIONS
Emotional volatility (anger, grief, excitement) can hijack rational decision-making. Rate: low (neutral), medium (distracted), high (volatile). Call out emotional state in the cockpit and reenter the DECIDE loop.
STEP 03 OF 04 · PREFLIGHT RISK
PAVE CHECKLIST
Systematically assess Pilot, Aircraft, enVironment, and External Pressures before and during flight to build a robust risk-management habit.
P — PILOT
Centers on your personal readiness and proficiency. IMSAFE check, recent training, currency with aircraft type, avionics, and emergency procedures, and personal minimums (e.g., crosswind limits, night takeoff minima).
A — AIRCRAFT
Addresses condition and performance capabilities. Verify airworthiness documents, preflight inspection (fuel, oil, control surfaces, tires), weight & balance, and POH performance charts for takeoff, climb, cruise, and landing.
V — eNVIRONMENT
Examines all external flight conditions: weather, terrain, airports, airspace. Obtain full weather briefing (METARs, TAFs, winds aloft, AIRMETs/SIGMETs), NOTAMs, TFRs, terrain clearance, and plan alternates.
E — EXTERNAL PRESSURES
Human factor influences: passenger expectations, time pressures, business demands, personal ambitions. Build contingency plans. Establish a no-guilt Go/No-Go decision point and stick to it.
STEP 04 OF 04 · IN-FLIGHT DECISION
ENHANCED DECIDE MODEL
A dynamic, self-correcting decision cycle: Detect → Evaluate → Consider → Integrate → Decide → Execute & Reassess. Complete all six steps.
1 DETECT
Maintain continuous, methodical vigilance. Systematic scan of every flight domain: external (weather cells, traffic, terrain), internal (fuel state, systems), and personal (IMSAFE). Cross-check instruments every 20–30 seconds. Log emerging hazards on your kneeboard.
2 EVALUATE
Two-dimensional GRM risk assessment: Severity (how big an upset or injury could be) × Likelihood (how probable is that outcome). The system computes your Risk Score automatically from the matrix.
Select Severity and Likelihood to compute score.
3 CONSIDER
Brainstorm all viable courses of action — do not lock onto the first fix. Generate at least 3 workable mitigations or alternatives. Engage your crew or passengers for additional input.
    0 options added (minimum 3 required)
    4 INTEGRATE
    Merge chosen mitigations into a coherent flight plan. Balance against aircraft limits (weight & balance, climb/descent performance), external constraints (airspace, fuel, approach minima), and sequence tasks into a logical flow.
    5 DECIDE
    Select the single best integrated plan and commit to it. A clear yes/no commitment. Communicate promptly: "I'm diverting to MXY. Heading 180, squawk 1200, time enroute 12 min." A decision is only good if it's shared and executed without hesitation.
    6 EXECUTE & REASSESS
    Put your decision into action, then monitor outcomes. Verify plan's effect on safety and schedule. Five minutes after a diversion, run another PAVE scan. If a new hazard emerges, restart DECIDE.
